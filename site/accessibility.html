Accessibility

These tools are for brains that mainstream interfaces routinely fail. So accessibility here is not a checklist bolted on at the end — it is the bar every page has to clear. This page says what is built in, what is not, and where the limits are.

What every page does

  • Keyboard-first. Everything interactive works with Tab, Shift+Tab, and Enter. A skip link jumps straight to the content, focus is always visible, and Escape cancels the transition timer.
  • Status you can hear. Timers carry role="timer" so screen readers treat them as clocks, not chatter. Counts, phase changes, and wind-down warnings are announced through polite live regions.
  • No motion ambushes. The breathing pacer goes static under prefers-reduced-motion, and every toolkit has a manual reduce-motion toggle that does the same.
  • Readable text. Body text meets WCAG AA contrast (4.5:1). No page opens internal links in new tabs — you stay where you are.
  • Your state survives. Half-finished sessions, dumps, and timers persist in this browser across reloads. Interruptions are normal here, not punished.

Comfort settings on every toolkit

Each toolkit has a small toolbar (bottom-right) with four controls. Set them once — they carry across every toolkit on this device:

  • Text size — Normal, Large, Extra large.
  • Dyslexia-friendly font — OpenDyslexic, loaded only when you switch it on.
  • Reduce motion — stops scaling, pulsing, and transitions.
  • Read aloud — reads the page back, best natural voice the browser offers.
  • Voice input — a mic button sits next to every text field, including the brain dump box.
Principle
Meet the brain where it is.
A page you can't comfortably read, in a moment when reading is already hard, isn't neutral — it's another demand. Multiple ways in lower that demand instead of adding to it.

Known limits

  • English only. No translations yet.
  • Keyboard- and code-audited, not screen-reader tested. The semantics are built to the standard (landmarks, labels, live regions, timer roles), but no full pass with VoiceOver, TalkBack, or NVDA has happened yet.
  • Voice features depend on the browser. Dictation and read-aloud need browser support; dictation sends audio to your browser's speech service (see Privacy).
  • Dyslexia font comes from a CDN the first time you enable it — it needs a connection that once.
A gentle note
None of this is medical advice or a diagnosis, and no tool here replaces a therapist, doctor, or your own judgement. These are scaffolding — external structure for a brain that does better with structure outside its own head.
